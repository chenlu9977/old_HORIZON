日記的重要性

4/1/2024

0 Comments

 
四月二號你好，
現在住在沖繩。
剛來的時候我媽是一起來的
反正我們一起去看了鯨魚 (到後面會很暈，不推)
然後一起去了美麗海水族館
凡正我媽覺得很感動(他來第二次了，上次應該是30年前)
以前我來的時候看得很仔細，看很久
這次和媽媽來就看得比較快。
在鯨鯊的缸子前面坐很久
之後也沒甚麼事，去了outlet但這邊牌子沒有很多
omoromachi旁邊的DTS牌子比較多，但是是買了之後要在機場排隊取貨的，就覺得麻煩(我媽)
後來我媽就回去了，她在機場的時候我有覺得有點難過
然後 不知道為什麼她沒連上機場WIFI，就這樣失聯直到她回到台灣
那個下午我都在區公所辦文件，等超久的
拿到住民票之後
給國際交流的負責的老頭(他看起來超老)，這邊人都工作到超老，身體健康是好事啦
但是也給人一種她們是不是不太會理財的感覺
而且看他不太會電腦打字，一個字一個字慢慢打的感覺實在覺得辛苦耶
跟他說我想搬家搬到那些台灣交換生住的地方去
反正他講話含滷蛋的，聽不清楚到底我沒辦法搬過去的原因是甚麼
聯絡幾間月租公寓的都沒有給我回信
阿超貴
後來下定決心要跟房東說我不能住這裡
他問我原因，我就說我長太高了，跟這個建築的size不合
他就說附近他有另一間
雖然交通沒有這間方便
不過就房子新一點，西式的，應該是沒有拉門了
交通之後應該會買電動自行車
應該還行吧，恩
補述:
阿昨天還有跟這邊的教授見面，他還超親切的導覽美術棟的空間和教室
還把我介紹給每個老師，有一個老師感覺超嗨親切的，甚至到有點令我害怕

昨天後來處理完學校的事，就在煩惱住處的事(房東那時還沒有叫我去住另一個地方)，走去教授說有宿舍的教會，凡正走來走去，旁邊英語教室的外國人就問我在找啥
我就問他有住的地方嗎
他也很親切，就幫我打聽了一下
紐約來的，他叫比爾威廉斯
他女兒的照片看起來超哈美國的
大概這樣吧
晚一點我要去房東說的另外一個地方看看了

​Hello April 2nd,
I’m currently living in Okinawa.
My mom came with me when I first arrived.
Anyway, we went to see the whales together (got pretty dizzy towards the end, wouldn’t recommend).
Then, we visited the beautiful aquarium.
My mom was really moved (it was her second visit, the last time must have been about 30 years ago).
When I came before, I looked at everything very carefully and for a long time.
This time, with my mom, we went through it a bit faster.
We sat in front of the whale shark tank for a long time.
After that, not much happened. We went to an outlet but there weren’t many brands available here.
Near Omoromachi, there were more brands at DTS, but you had to queue at the airport to pick up what you bought, which was a bit of a hassle (according to my mom).
Later, my mom went back home. I felt a bit sad when she was at the airport.
Then, for some reason, she couldn’t connect to the airport WiFi, so we lost contact until she got back to Taiwan.
That afternoon, I was at the municipal office handling some paperwork, waiting for ages.
After getting my residence certificate,
I gave it to the guy in charge of international exchange (he looked really old). People here work till they are really old, which I guess is good because it means they're healthy.
But it also makes you wonder if they're not that good at managing their finances.
And seeing him struggle with typing on the computer, hitting the keys one by one, really made me feel it was tough.
I told him I wanted to move to where the Taiwanese exchange students live.
Anyway, his explanation was muddled, and I couldn’t clearly understand why I couldn’t move there.
I contacted several monthly rental apartments, but none got back to me.
Ah, they're so expensive.
Eventually, I decided to tell the landlord I couldn’t stay here.
He asked me why, and I said I was too tall, and the size of the building didn't suit me.
He mentioned he had another place nearby.
Though it's not as convenient in terms of transportation,
the house is newer, more Western-style, probably without sliding doors.
I'll probably get an electric bike for commuting.
Should be fine, I guess.
Addition:
Ah, I also met with a professor here yesterday. He was super friendly, showing me around the art building and classrooms.
He introduced me to every teacher, one of whom was so overly enthusiastic it was almost scary.

After sorting out school stuff yesterday, I was worrying about where to live (the landlord hadn’t yet told me to move to another place). I went to a church the professor mentioned had dormitories, walking back and forth, when a foreigner from the English class next door asked what I was looking for.
I asked him if he knew any places to stay.
He was really kind and asked around for me.
He’s from New York, named Bill Williams.
His daughter's photos look super American.
That's about it.
I’ll be checking out the other place the landlord mentioned later.
